Policy vocabulary.
Indicators (conditions are evaluated on EVERY unit, at every step):
- food_per_occupant: food in stock in the cell, per occupant
- water_per_occupant: drinkable water in stock in the cell, per occupant
- oxygen_per_occupant: oxygen in stock in the cell, per occupant
- ice_per_occupant: collectable ice (stock plus surface), per occupant
- material_per_occupant: construction_material in the cell's warehouse, per occupant
- minerals_per_occupant: minerals in the cell's seam, per occupant
- power_coverage: power coverage: available energy divided by the load of the cell's installations (1.0 = demand is met exactly)
- structure_integrity: average maintenance state of the cell's structures, from 0 to 1
- occupants: how many colonists are in the cell
Operators: < and >.
Weightable categories: build, explore, life, resources, sustenance.
What each category covers:
- sustenance: drinking, eating, collecting ice, farming and harvesting food. The category of hunger and thirst.
- resources: collecting minerals from the deposit and construction_material from the cell's warehouse. WARNING: collecting construction_material does not PRODUCE any — it moves it from the warehouse into the colonists' pockets, and construction already draws directly from the warehouse. New material comes from the deposits (and from ISRU, if active); minerals from the seam. Weighting `resources` in order to 'have more material' is therefore wasted work: it helps only where the colonists must carry it with them (moves, foundations).
- build: erecting structures (greenhouses, habitats, panels, labs...) and maintaining them. A structure whose per-inhabitant ratio is already satisfied is not built anyway.
- life: rest, care, recovery. It produces nothing by itself.
- explore: moving and exploring, that is, founding new settlements.
A weight is a multiplier between 0.25 and 4 (1.0 = no change; below 1 discourages, above 1 encourages).
Weights are RELATIVE among the categories: the scores are renormalised, so raising three categories to x2-x3 while leaving the others at 1 amounts to PENALISING the latter, which lose share in the sampling. A category you want to protect (for example explore, without which nobody founds new cells) must be raised too, not left at 1.
At most 8 rules. For each unit the FIRST rule that fires is the one that counts, in the order in which you write them; the others are ignored. A rule with "if": null always fires and closes the chain.
